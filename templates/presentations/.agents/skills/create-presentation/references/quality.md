# Presentation quality

## Story

- Design for the audience's decision, not for topic coverage.
- Establish a beginning, development, and resolution. Make adjacent slides
  feel causally connected.
- Give each slide one dominant idea. Move secondary detail into notes, builds,
  or another slide.
- Prefer concrete evidence, examples, comparisons, and demonstrations over
  slogans.
- Keep claims and media attributable. Never invent citations or customer data.

## Visual direction

- Derive the visual language from the topic, customer brand, audience, and
  setting. Never apply a house aesthetic by reflex.
- Establish reusable type, color, spacing, and shape rules before polishing
  individual pages.
- Build hierarchy through scale, position, contrast, and whitespace. Avoid
  card grids as a default composition.
- Vary slide density and rhythm: statements, evidence, diagrams, comparisons,
  images, demonstrations, and quiet transitions should not all look alike.
- Use motion to preserve context, reveal causality, or direct attention. Avoid
  motion that exists only to advertise the framework.

## Media and accessibility

- Prefer local optimized assets for reliable production builds.
- Write useful alt text for meaningful images; mark decorative elements so
  they do not compete with the message.
- Maintain readable type and strong contrast at presentation distance.
- Never stretch, distort, or crop away the subject of an image.
- Respect licenses and attribution requirements for images, fonts, themes,
  add-ons, and embedded media.

## Review

- Inspect the first state and every click state of every slide.
- Check text overflow, unexpected scrollbars, clipping, line breaks, safe
  margins, contrast, focus order, and keyboard navigation.
- Confirm diagrams and code are legible from the back of a room.
- Confirm presenter notes support delivery without repeating slide copy.
- Test the immutable production build, not only the development server.
